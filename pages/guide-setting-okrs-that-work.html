Setting OKRs That Actually Work

A practical framework for goals that drive real outcomes

Overview

What Makes a Good OKR?

OKRs - Objectives and Key Results - were invented at Intel by Andy Grove and made famous by Google. Set a clear, inspiring Objective (what you want to achieve) and pair it with 2–4 measurable Key Results (how you'll know you got there).

The OKR test: Read your Objective out loud. Does it make someone lean forward? Now read your Key Results. Can you unambiguously answer "did we hit this?" at the end of the quarter? If no to either - you have a to-do list with aspirational language, not an OKR.
Preparation

Before You Write a Single Goal

1
What are the 3–5 things that must be true for this year to be a success?
Not everything - the 3–5 things. If your CEO cannot answer this in one sentence per theme, your organisation isn't ready for OKRs.
2
Are you using OKRs for alignment or accountability?
Alignment OKRs are shared across teams. Accountability OKRs are individually owned. Mixing both without clarity creates the worst of both worlds.
3
What is the grading convention?
Google uses a 0–1 scale where 0.7 is considered a success. Decide this before launch and don't change it mid-cycle.
⚠️ The cascade trap: Don't cascade OKRs top-down like a waterfall. Best practice: "top-down context, bottom-up goals."
The Process

Writing OKRs Step-by-Step

1
Write the Objective - make it a rallying cry, not a task
Bad: "Improve customer satisfaction." Good: "Become the most trusted onboarding experience in our category by Q4."
2
Write 2–4 Key Results - outcomes, not activities
Bad: "Launch new onboarding flow." Good: "Increase Day-30 retention from 61% to 78%." The launch is an activity. The retention improvement is a Key Result.
3
Apply the "so what" test to each Key Result
If we hit this number and nothing else changed, would the Objective be meaningfully advanced? If no - it's a proxy metric.
4
Set the baseline before setting the target
"Increase NPS from 32 to 55" is valid. "Increase NPS to 55" is not - no baseline means no way to assess ambition or fairness.
5
Run a weekly or bi-weekly check-in, not a quarterly one
OKRs without cadenced check-ins are just goal documents. 15 minutes. Three questions: status, confidence score, one blocker.
Common Pitfalls

The Most Common OKR Mistakes

  • Too many OKRs. More than 3 Objectives and 9 Key Results means you have priorities, not OKRs.
  • Tying OKRs directly to bonuses. The moment OKRs are financially consequential, people start sandbagging.
  • Set-and-forget culture. OKRs that aren't reviewed regularly become irrelevant within six weeks.
  • Writing KRs for tasks you'd do anyway. "Complete Q3 product roadmap" is not a Key Result - it's your job.
Implementation Checklist

OKR Health Checklist

Tick off each step as you complete it. Your progress is saved on this device.

Company-level Objectives are set by leadership and communicated before team OKR-writing begins
Each Objective is qualitative, inspiring, and achievable within the OKR period
Each team has no more than 3 Objectives and 4 Key Results per Objective
Every Key Result is an outcome metric, not an activity or deliverable
Every Key Result has a clear baseline number and a stretch target
Grading convention (0–1 scale, RAG, % completion) is agreed and communicated
OKRs are not directly tied to bonus or compensation decisions
Weekly or bi-weekly check-in cadence is scheduled for the full OKR period
At least one Key Result per Objective would be uncomfortable to miss
OKRs are visible across the team - not siloed in individual documents
